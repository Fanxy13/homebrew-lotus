# Voice-over (English)

About 6 minutes at a calm pace (130 words a minute). Pauses in [brackets]. Scene numbers match `storyboard.md`.

**1 – Cold open (0:00)**
Every terminal starts empty. [pause] A blinking cursor, and nothing else. [pause]
Lotus changes what you see when you open it.

**2 – Title (0:12)**
This is Lotus 3.0. [pause] Your terminal, reimagined – on macOS, and now on Linux.

**3 – The start screen (0:22)**
Open a new window and Lotus greets you. The system, the time, what is playing, the weather if you like –
in the colors of a theme you choose. [pause] It is your terminal, still. Lotus only adds a door.
Type a slash, and a command opens: weather, a clock, your music, your apps. [pause]
And every one of them is a plain word, in plain text. Nothing is hidden.

**4 – Install on a Mac (1:00)**
On a Mac, one line. [pause] A short setup asks for your name, your language and a theme –
then it shows you what it is about to save, and waits for a yes.

**5 – Install on Linux (1:30)**
On Linux it is the same line – with a different shell at the end. [pause]
There is no administrator password in it. Lotus lives in your home folder.
Its engine is zsh. If you do not have it, Lotus says so, shows you the exact command, and asks first.
[pause] You keep your shell, too: Lotus loads into bash as well as zsh.

**6 – The same on both (2:15)**
The commands are the same. The settings are the same plain files. The pets are the same.
Where a system cannot do something, Lotus tells you in words instead of failing quietly –
and the website has a table of what works where, and only what is tested is marked as working.

**7 – The AI terminal (2:50)**
Type slash-ai, and you can work with an AI in the terminal. Use Apple Intelligence on a Mac, Claude with your own key,
or a model on your own computer through Ollama. [pause]
You decide what it may do. In ask mode, every command waits for you. In auto mode, it runs what is safe –
and asks when it is not sure, and tells you why. [pause] Your keys stay in the Keychain or your desktop's keyring.
They are never in a file you could share by mistake.

**8 – Images (3:40)**
Slash-img-convert. Drop a folder of photos in, choose WebP, and watch the lotus bloom as they are converted.
[pause] On a Mac it uses Apple's own image engine. On Linux, ImageMagick. The originals are never touched.

**9 – Pets (4:10)**
And because a terminal should have some life in it: pets. [pause]
Feed them with a little mental arithmetic. Two minutes, normal difficulty. The more you get right, the more they eat.

**10 – Plugins (4:40)**
Plugins add commands, tools for the AI and lines on the start screen.
A plugin tells you what it may do before you install it, and the limited ones run in a sandbox –
on a Mac with the system's sandbox, on Linux with bubblewrap. If there is no sandbox, the plugin is refused. Never run unprotected.

**11 – Under the hood (5:10)**
Behind this is a platform layer: the commands do not know which system they run on.
The tests run in containers on Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora and Arch, and on a Mac. [pause]
What is not tested is not claimed.

**12 – Windows (5:40)**
Windows is next. A native PowerShell version for Windows 11 is in development. It is not in this release.

**13 – Outro (5:50)**
Lotus is free and open source. The link is below. [pause]
Open a new terminal. And let it bloom.
